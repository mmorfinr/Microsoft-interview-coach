What this HM screen is really testing

This is the hiring-manager screen, not the technical loop. Expect ~30–45 minutes, mostly about your background at a high level, your motivation, and 2–3 behavioral stories. The interviewer is checking three things: can you operate credibly with enterprise engineering teams, can you drive a complex program without formal authority, and will you fit Microsoft's culture. Save deep technical whiteboarding for later rounds — lead with judgment and outcomes here.

Microsoft's core competencies (map every story to one of these)

  • Drive for Results — tenacity, ownership, quantified outcomes. Your strongest card: the CNDP migration that took deployments from 200 to 10,000+/month and produced $2.2B+ in annual savings.
  • Customer Focus — grounding decisions in customer/partner needs. Your OEM Technical Account Manager work and escalation-to-platform-fix pattern at T-Mobile map directly here.
  • Influencing for Impact / Collaboration without authority — this JD explicitly calls out "no direct authority" across MCAPS, customer engineering, and Azure service teams. Have a clean multi-stakeholder story ready.
  • Accountability — owning a program end-to-end, including when it's off track, and communicating that transparently.
  • Technical credibility — not coding depth, but comfort in engineering conversations. Your multi-vendor cloud/network integration background (Ericsson, Nokia, Mavenir, IoT, eSIM) is your proof point.

How this JD maps to your resume

  • Customer Technical Engagement → OEM Technical Account Manager & Program Lead role; primary technical contact for strategic partners.
  • Program Planning & Execution → T-Mobile CNDP migration; nationwide infrastructure modernization program.
  • Escalation & Issue Management → "Collaborated with engineering teams to resolve customer issues, manage escalations" bullet — this is a direct line to their JD language, use it almost verbatim in a story.
  • Platform Feedback & Improvement → "Implemented platform improvements based on customer issues and engineering feedback."
  • Cloud infrastructure domain depth → multi-vendor cloud infrastructure (Ericsson, Nokia, Mavenir, IoT, eSIM, VTT) reducing integration testing time 25%.

Two things to shore up before the call

Your resume is telecom/T-Mobile-and-AT&T-heavy; it doesn't say "Azure" or "cloud-native SaaS at hyperscaler" anywhere except CNDP. Be ready for "you haven't worked at a hyperscaler — how do you see your experience translating?" Have a 20-second bridge answer ready: distributed systems at national carrier scale, multi-vendor cloud-native platforms, and enterprise customer engineering are the transferable core — the domain changes, the operating pattern doesn't. Second: your MS in AI Engineering (WGU, expected Nov 2026) and Microsoft AI Product Manager certification are good signals of growth mindset — mention them briefly if asked about continued learning.
